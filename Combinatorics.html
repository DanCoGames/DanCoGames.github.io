Concept Masterclass

1. Fundamental Counting Principle

If one event can occur in ways and a second independent event can occur in ways, then the total number of ways both events can occur together is their product.

Example: If you have 3 shirts and 4 pairs of pants, you can create different outfits.

2. Permutations (Order Matters)

A permutation is an arrangement of items in a specific order. Selecting a combination to a safe or determining 1st, 2nd, and 3rd place in a race are examples where order changes the outcome.

Example: How many ways can 3 runners finish 1st, 2nd, and 3rd out of 8 competitors?

3. Combinations (Order Doesn't Matter)

A combination is a selection of items where the order does not matter. Forming a committee or drawing a hand of cards are examples. We divide permutations by to remove duplicate arrangements.

Example: How many 3-person committees can be formed from a group of 8 people?

4. Basic Probability

Probability measures the likelihood of an event occurring. It is the ratio of successful outcomes to the total number of possible outcomes. Combinatorics is heavily used to calculate these outcomes.

Example: Winning a lottery where you pick 6 correct numbers out of 49.
